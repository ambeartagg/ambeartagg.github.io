UX Design • UX Research

You.Me.We.All

Redesigning a mental wellness resource website to create a calmer, more guided, and accessible experience for users seeking support.

Role

UX Designer & Researcher

Focus

Navigation · Accessibility · Resource Discoverability

Tools

Figma · UX Research · Prototyping

Project Overview

You.Me.We.All is a mental wellness resource platform designed to connect users with information, support, and community resources. For this project, I redesigned the website experience with a focus on helping users understand where to begin and find resources that are relevant to their needs.

Through my initial research and exploration, I identified several opportunities to improve the experience. Important resources could be difficult to find, large amounts of information competed for attention, and users could be unsure about which pathway was most relevant to them.

My goal was to create an experience that felt calmer, more approachable, and easier to navigate while still communicating trust and providing access to critical support.

Research & Discovery

I began the redesign by examining how a user might move through the existing experience. I created a target map to understand the different stages of the user's journey, from entering the website through discovering and learning about resources to taking action.

Target map exploring the user's journey from entering the website to finding relevant support.

Mapping the experience helped me identify areas where users could become uncertain, overwhelmed, or unsure about what to do next. This gave me a foundation for identifying opportunities for improvement.

Unclear Entry Points

First-time users may not immediately know where they should begin based on their needs or situation.

Information Overload

Large amounts of text and equally weighted information can make important resources harder to identify.

Hidden Resources

Important resources and actions could be difficult to locate when they were placed farther down the page.

Navigation Overload

A large amount of navigation content can make it harder for users to determine which option is most relevant.

How Might We?

These observations led me to develop a series of How Might We questions to guide the redesign. I used them to frame the problems as opportunities and explore different possible solutions.

Guide

How might we guide first-time users to the most relevant starting point based on their needs?

Prioritize

How might we make key actions and critical resources immediately visible?

Reduce Overwhelm

How might we create a calmer, less busy, and more emotionally supportive visual experience?

Simplify

How might we reduce text density and simplify navigation without losing important information or trust?

Create Clearer Pathways

How might we prioritize important content and resources so users can quickly understand what to do next?

Exploring Solutions

Before creating a polished interface, I explored several possible solutions through sketches. I focused on ways to make support more immediately accessible, reduce cognitive overload, and help users understand where to go next.

Early sketches exploring different approaches to navigation, support, and resource discovery.

Super Vote Solution

This concept prioritized immediate access to support by placing a prominent action for users seeking help at the top of the experience.

Calmer First Impression

This approach reduced the amount of text presented at once and focused the experience around a primary action to reduce cognitive overload.

Resource Categories

This concept allowed users to choose the type of resources they were looking for through clear, organized categories.

Clear Resource Context

This approach used prominent headings and supporting links to make the type of resources available immediately clear.

From Sketches to Interface

I then translated my ideas into an initial Figma mockup. This version allowed me to begin testing how the different ideas worked together as a cohesive website experience.

Initial Figma mockup exploring the redesigned homepage, parent and caregiver experience, and support pathway.

Emotional Entry Point

The homepage was designed to immediately make users feel supported and understood.

Role-Based Navigation

Users could identify the pathway most relevant to them, reducing uncertainty and overwhelm.

Visible Crisis Support

Crisis support remained accessible without dominating the primary experience.

Feedback & Iteration

After creating the initial prototype, I received feedback from my professor as well as classmates and friends. I wanted to understand not only whether the interface was usable, but also how the design made people feel.

I asked participants about their first impressions, whether the website felt relevant to them, how easily they could identify what to do next, and whether anything felt confusing, overwhelming, or missing.

What I Heard

Participants generally found the layout organized and easy to understand. One participant noted that the clear sections helped create a feeling that they could complete their tasks quickly.

A Remaining Opportunity

Some participants felt that, while support was discoverable, they might still need a moment to decide where to begin.

Stronger Guidance

Feedback suggested that stronger visual cues and clearer primary actions could make the experience feel more guided.

More Context

Users also wanted more information about what they would find within different sections before clicking deeper into the experience.

Design Iterations

I used this feedback to refine the final prototype. The first major iteration focused on improving user guidance and navigation clarity. I strengthened calls to action, clarified descriptions, and created a more intentional visual hierarchy.

The second major iteration focused on providing more context before users navigated deeper into the website. I added topic previews, featured resources, descriptive text, and resource imagery to make the experience more informative and transparent.

Final Prototype

The Final Design

The final prototype incorporates the feedback and observations from throughout the redesign process. The experience is organized around clearer pathways, more approachable visual design, and easier access to relevant resources.

01 — You’re Not Alone

The redesigned homepage creates a supportive first impression while giving users clear pathways based on their needs. Parents, educators, and individuals seeking support can immediately identify the pathway that best fits their situation.

Final homepage design with role-based entry points and visible access to crisis support.

02 — For Parents & Caregivers

This page provides a more personalized experience for parents and caregivers. Topic-based categories help users quickly locate information relevant to their situation, while featured resources provide additional context before users navigate deeper into the site.

Final Parents & Caregivers page with topic categories and featured resources.

03 — I Need Support

The I Need Support page was designed for users who may already be feeling stressed or emotionally overwhelmed. Three simplified pathways—finding resources, talking to someone, and finding community—help users identify a manageable next step.

I also introduced an emotional check-in that allows users to indicate how they are feeling and receive more personalized guidance.

Final support pathway with simplified support options and an emotional check-in.

Key Design Decisions

Calm & Approachable

Soft neutral tones, rounded cards, consistent spacing, and reduced visual density create a more welcoming experience.

Guided Entry Points

Clear user pathways help people quickly identify where they belong and what they can do next.

Discoverable Resources

Topic categories, previews, imagery, and descriptions help users understand what resources are available before navigating deeper.

Reflection

This project reinforced the importance of designing not only for usability, but also for how an experience makes people feel. Because the website focuses on mental wellness and support, reducing cognitive load and creating clear pathways became especially important.

Through research, iteration, and feedback, I learned how changes to hierarchy, navigation, and content presentation can significantly affect how approachable an experience feels. I also gained a better understanding of how feedback from different users can reveal opportunities that may not be immediately obvious during the initial design process.

Future development could explore additional user testing, mobile optimization, expanded accessibility features, and more personalized resource recommendations.
